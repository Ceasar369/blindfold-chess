# Legal moves, beyond reach

A square a piece reaches is not always a legal move.

List the squares its movement reaches.

Strike out those its own pieces block.

Picture each move made. Is its own king then attacked?

A king leaves its square: picture that square empty.

Example: White's king on g1, Black's rook on e1. The king may not step to h1: the rook's rank runs on through g1.

A pawn may shield its king from a rook, bishop or queen. Stepping off that line leaves the king open.

A pinned pawn may still move along that line.

Gradually see these at a glance.

Proven: FIDE Laws of Chess, Article 3.9.2. Checking each move this way is my preferred approach.
