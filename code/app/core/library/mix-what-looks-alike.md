# Mix what looks alike

Mix kinds of problem that look alike, such as a rook's, bishop's and queen's lines.

Learn plain labels, such as square names, one set at a time.

Mixing feels harder and works better.

Proven: Brunmair and Richter 2019, a meta-analysis of 59 studies: mixing beat one kind at a time (g 0.42), most when the kinds look alike; for words, one at a time won (g -0.39); Kornell and Bjork 2008: learners rated mixing worse though it worked better. None used chess. Applying it to chess is my preferred approach.
